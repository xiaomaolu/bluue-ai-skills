# Style Profile

Learn durable writing preferences from explicit user choices and edits.

This module should improve future content without copying prior sentences mechanically.

## Learn abstractions

Useful preferences include:

- short vs long openings
- opinion-first vs context-first
- sentence length
- paragraph density
- preferred level of informality
- appetite for numbers
- use of rhetorical questions
- emoji frequency
- hashtag frequency
- preferred angle families
- disliked clichés
- preferred terminology
- code-switching tolerance
- platform-specific differences

Example:

AI draft:
> Apple may redefine the foldable market.

User edit:
> Apple is late to foldables. That might be the advantage.

Possible learned preferences:

- shorter opening
- thesis earlier
- plain language
- restrained claim strength
- avoid “redefine”

## Do not overfit

One edit is weak evidence. Prefer repeated or explicit preferences.

Do not store a copied sentence as a template.

## Multilingual profiles

Maintain language-specific preferences when necessary.

A user may prefer:

- concise English X posts
- denser Chinese LinkedIn copy
- casual Vietnamese Facebook wording

Do not assume style transfers identically across languages.

## Sensitive information

Do not infer or store sensitive personal traits. Style learning concerns writing behavior only.

## Conflict resolution

Priority:

1. current explicit request
2. platform-specific explicit preference
3. language-specific style preference
4. general style profile
5. platform defaults
