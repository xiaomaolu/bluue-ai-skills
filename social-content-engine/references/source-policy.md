# Source Policy

## Source tiers

### Tier A — primary / authoritative
- official company announcements
- regulators and government
- court or legislative material
- academic papers
- product documentation
- original datasets

### Tier B — high-quality established reporting
- Reuters
- Bloomberg
- Financial Times
- Wall Street Journal
- major established technology/business publications

### Tier C — specialist sources
- industry publications
- specialist analysts
- reputable technical blogs
- niche research firms

### Tier D — community / social
- X
- Reddit
- forums
- individual blogs
- comments

## Usage

Facts:
prefer A/B.

Technical interpretation:
A/C may be strongest.

Community sentiment:
C/D.

“What people are saying”:
D is appropriate, but describe it as discussion or examples, not population-level truth.

## Recency

For current topics, prioritize sources matching the requested time window.

Do not use an old evergreen page as evidence for “latest” when a current source is required.

## Disagreement

When credible sources disagree:

- preserve uncertainty
- attribute the different claims
- lower confidence
- avoid choosing the more dramatic version without evidence

## Social sources

A public post can support:

> Person X said Y.

It may not support:

> Y is objectively true.

Domain-filtered web search can miss posts and deleted/limited pages. Do not call it complete.

## Quotes

Prefer paraphrase. Use short quotes only when the exact wording matters.

Do not reproduce large portions of articles, posts, transcripts, or paywalled text.

## Numbers

Record:

- value
- unit
- period
- geography
- source
- whether the number is estimated

Do not mix statistics from incompatible periods without saying so.
